How to Travel With Your Dog by Car

Everything you need for a safe, comfortable road trip — from restraints to rest stops to what to pack.

📅 Updated 2025 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ By Hopetsy Team

Taking your dog on a road trip is one of the best parts of pet ownership — the excited tail wags, the new smells, the photos. But without the right preparation, it can also be stressful and even dangerous. This guide walks through everything you need to travel safely and comfortably with your dog by car.

1. Secure Your Dog in the Car

The most important rule of car travel with a dog is restraint. An unrestrained dog is a distraction and a danger — in a sudden stop, even a small dog becomes a projectile. There are two main options:

  • Car seatbelt leash: A short, padded leash that clips to your dog's harness and the seatbelt buckle. Great for well-behaved dogs who like to sit and watch the road.
  • Pet carrier: The safest option, especially for smaller dogs. Secure the carrier with the seatbelt or place it on the floor behind the front seats.

Never let your dog ride in the front passenger seat with an active airbag — the force of deployment can be fatal for pets.

💡 Pro tip: A car seatbelt leash is one of the most affordable safety upgrades you can make. It costs less than a single vet visit and could save your dog's life.

2. Pack the Essentials

Here's a packing checklist for a dog road trip. You don't need everything on every trip, but having a dedicated "dog travel bag" makes leaving easy:

  • Food & bowls: Pre-measure food into baggies. A collapsible bowl saves huge amounts of space.
  • Water & water bottle: Bring water from home — some dogs get upset stomachs from unfamiliar tap water on the road.
  • Leash & waste bags: Always have extras. Rest stops and trailheads may not stock them.
  • Favorite toy or blanket: Familiar smells help anxious dogs settle in unfamiliar places.
  • Vaccination records: Some hotels, campgrounds, and borders require proof.

If you're planning a road trip with your dog, a portable water bottle and collapsible bowl can help reduce the amount of space your pet gear takes up — while making sure your dog stays hydrated and fed at every stop.

3. Plan Your Rest Stops

Dogs need a break every 2–3 hours — to stretch, relieve themselves, and drink water. Plan your route with rest stops in mind:

  • Look for rest areas with grass, not just gas stations on concrete.
  • Avoid leaving your dog alone in the car, especially in warm weather. Car interiors can reach dangerous temperatures in minutes.
  • Keep the leash on at every stop — even well-trained dogs can spook at highway noise.

4. Keep Your Dog Hydrated

Dehydration sneaks up fast on the road. Signs include excessive panting, dry gums, lethargy, and sunken eyes. To prevent it:

  • Offer water at every rest stop — don't wait for your dog to seem thirsty.
  • Use a no-spill travel water bottle for one-handed dispensing while walking.
  • In hot weather, freeze water bottles overnight so they melt slowly through the day.

5. Protect Your Car

Mud, fur, and the occasional accident are part of traveling with dogs. A waterproof seat cover catches all of it and can be pulled off and shaken clean at any stop. For heavy shedders, a full hammock-style cover also protects your doors.

6. Build a Travel Kit

The easiest way to be road-trip ready at all times? Assemble a dedicated travel kit that lives in your car. At minimum, include:

  • A collapsible silicone bowl
  • A portable water bottle
  • A car seatbelt leash
  • A waste bag holder

You can build a pre-bundled travel kit or assemble your own. Either way, the point is to never have to scramble to pack — you're always ready to go.

The Bottom Line

Traveling with your dog doesn't have to be complicated. With the right restraints, a packed travel bag, and a plan for rest stops, road trips become one of the best things you can do together. Start with the essentials — a safe restraint, a good bowl, a water bottle — and build from there.
